No account or sign-in is required, and there is nothing to unlock — tap Play vs
Computer or Two Players and the whole app is available on the first launch.

There are no in-app purchases and no advertising. Nothing in the app is for sale
and no feature is limited by a daily quota. The Game Review — every move graded,
an accuracy score per player and an evaluation graph — is the app's largest
feature and it is free and unlimited like the rest of it.

NO GAMBLING OF ANY KIND. Chess is a game of pure skill. Nothing is wagered,
nothing pays out, and the app has no StoreKit code and no networking code, so it
cannot transact with anything. The Chance-Based Activities section of the age
rating questionnaire is answered None, and the Gambling declaration in App
Information is No.

Fully offline. The chess engine is the app's own and runs on the device; there
are no packages, no third-party SDKs and no network requests of any kind. The
pieces are drawn from vector paths in code and the sounds are synthesized at
launch, so the app ships with no audio assets. The puzzles are a file inside the
bundle, read by the app and by its home-screen widget alike. Settings, a game in
progress, the archive of finished games, statistics, achievements and puzzle
progress are stored on the device only: the settings and the game in progress
in UserDefaults (declared in chess/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy, reason CA92.1), the
archive of finished games, the review cache and puzzle progress as JSON files in
the app's Application Support directory. The game clock, the chess clock and
Race the Clock measure elapsed time with the system uptime (declared in the same
file, reason 35F9.1); only differences between two readings are used, and they
never leave the device. PGN files are only ever exported
through the system share sheet and imported through the system file picker, at
the player's request.

Third-party content: the Staunton piece shapes are derived from the SVG chess
pieces drawn by Wikimedia Commons user Cburnett, used under the BSD licence. The
attribution, with the licence in full, is shown to players in the app under
Settings → Credits. The engine's piece-square tables are the Simplified
Evaluation Function by Tomasz Michniewski, from the Chess Programming Wiki
(CC BY-SA 3.0), credited in the same place. The tactics puzzles are a selection
from the Lichess puzzle database, which Lichess releases under CC0 (public
domain); they are credited under Settings → Credits and on the puzzle screen
anyway. Everything else — the search, the review, the guide, the clock, the
training tools, the sounds — is original.

How to play: from the menu choose Play vs Computer (five levels, and White,
Black or Random), Two Players on one device, or How to Play for the guide. On the
board, tap a piece and the dots show every square it may legally go to, then tap
one to play the move; dragging works too. The row under the board is Undo, then
Hint against the computer or Draw between two players, then Resign. The gear at
the top right holds five board themes, sounds, haptics, the legal-move dots and
the board coordinates; the arrows beside it flip the board. Leaving a game with
the chevron at the top left saves it, and it comes back under Continue in the
menu. When a game ends, Game Review opens from the result card, and every
finished game is kept in My Games, where tapping it and choosing Game Review
reviews it again.

The guide is nine chapters and 38 pages with 27 board diagrams, 15 of which are
playable: the dots on those are real legal moves asked from the engine, and
tapping one plays it.

Accessibility: every board square is a VoiceOver button that says its coordinate
and what stands on it, so a whole game can be played by ear. Dynamic Type is
supported up to the accessibility sizes and Reduce Motion is honored.

Localisation: English and Czech, switched by the device language. There is no
in-app language setting. Move notation follows the reader's language — Nf3 reads
Jf3 in Czech — while the notation stored on the device stays English.

Beyond the games themselves, the menu leads to: Puzzles (rated puzzles, themes,
a daily puzzle, Race the Clock, and positions from the player's own reviewed games),
Training (an opening trainer, an analysis board, a position editor and pasting in
a PGN or FEN), Chess Clock (a clock for a game on a real board) and My Games
(the archive, statistics, achievements and PGN import and export). A game can be
played on a clock — bullet to classical — from a Chess960 or odds position, and
under King of the Hill or Three-check rules; both setup screens offer it. The widget shows the daily puzzle and opens it.

The app supports iPhone (portrait) and iPad (portrait and landscape).
